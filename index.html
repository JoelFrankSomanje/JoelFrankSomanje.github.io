About

Most people learn networking to pass an exam.

I learned it to find out what actually breaks — and how to build past it. I'm a third-year Computer Networking Engineering student working out of Malawi, building the kind of enterprise infrastructure most people don't touch until years into a networking career: VLAN-segmented, OSPF-routed, redundant by design.

I'm not interested in a network that merely works. I want to know exactly how it fails, then make sure it doesn't — through the same discipline enterprise engineers use: segment first, route deliberately, secure by default, and test the failure before someone else finds it for you.

Local roots, global standard. I'm building here, in Malawi, but I'm building to a bar that travels anywhere.
